# PART 1: Brick by Brick competitors

## 1. The Systems Studio Co. (best match for your ops offer)
- URL: https://www.thesystemsstudioco.com/
- Serves: founders of founder-led businesses who are the bottleneck.
- Hero: "Design a Company That Doesn't Depend on You." Subhead: "Operational leadership and systems for founder-led businesses — so growth stops running through you."
- CTA: "Apply to Work Together" in hero and again near the bottom. A smaller text link in the hero offers a free quiz for people not ready to apply.
- Trust: one named CEO testimonial, phone and email visible, "reply within one business day." Thin on proof.
- Structure: hero, the problem (founder as approval system), the solution (4 outcomes), two services (audit, fractional COO), free "Vacation Test" quiz, testimonial, $147 template product, closing CTA.
- Pricing: only the $147 product is priced. Audit and COO are "apply."
- Lead magnet: **Vacation Test**, a free 20-question quiz ("Could your business survive two weeks without you?").
- Style (inferred): light, airy, big headings, stock-style photos.
- Notable: a clean ladder: free quiz, paid audit, ongoing work, with a cheap product in between. Same single idea (founder dependency) repeated everywhere.

## 2. OpsKings Operations Consulting
- URL: https://www.opskings.com/ops-consulting
- Serves: $5M-$100M companies, 10-100 people, founders without a COO. Says no to pre-revenue startups and solo operators.
- Hero: "Operations consulting for founders without a COO." Subhead: "You built the company. Now everything runs through you..."
- CTA: "Book an Audit" in hero and bottom; described as a no-pitch call.
- Trust: "130+ engagements," named founder testimonials with logos, stat callouts ("30-70% faster cycle times," "10-20 hours per week saved per department head").
- Structure: hero, results numbers, symptoms (5 pains), 3 pillars, 4-phase method (Audit, Map, Analyze, Execute), deliverables, interactive "Company Brain" demo, who it is / is not for, testimonials, FAQ, final CTA.
- Pricing: none, but anchors against a $200K-$400K full-time COO and says the audit takes 2 weeks, 3-5 calls.
- Lead magnet: none (call only).
- Notable: the "not a fit" section and the numbered method make it feel serious. Bigger than you (a team), so use as a structure model only.

## 3. Asamby Consulting (fractional COO)
- URL: https://asamby.com/
- Serves: founder-led service businesses, 10-100 employees, $2-10M revenue.
- Hero: "Unlock growth with a Fractional COO." Subhead: "We manage your team day-to-day, build scalable infrastructure and execute your strategy. So you can focus on growth."
- CTA: "Book a Free Discovery Call," repeated 5+ times down the page.
- Trust: client logo strip, 5 testimonials incl. video, Clutch/Google/Upwork badges, 30-day money-back guarantee, "we onboard 1-2 clients per quarter."
- Structure: hero + logos, "Is this you?" pains, 6 services, proof + guarantee, 2-minute quiz, 4-step "how it works," COO vs full-time comparison, team, testimonials, FAQ (6), booking, contact form.
- Pricing: none shown; says month-to-month, 30 days' notice, 6-18 month typical.
- Lead magnet: "Do you actually need a Fractional COO?" quiz.
- Notable: guarantee plus a promise of a real human answer ("No AI slop").

## 4. Savvy Assistant Co. (solo fractional ops)
- URL: https://www.savvyassistantco.com/fractional-operations-manager
- Serves: solo founders who have outgrown doing everything.
- Hero: "A fractional operations manager for solopreneurs who've outgrown doing it all themselves."
- CTA: "Book a discovery call" (Calendly) in hero, nav, and closing; plus a chat widget lead form.
- Trust: weak (a blog-list feature, "work directly with one operator," tool logos). No testimonials visible.
- Structure: hero, two personas, two offers (2-6 week Sprint, ongoing support), tools, 8-question FAQ, closing CTA "Stop being the bottleneck in your own business."
- Pricing: separate "Investment" page; hours range (5-20/week) mentioned.
- Notable: the FAQ answers "how are you different from a virtual assistant?" That is the single most useful objection to answer for a solo.

## 5. Rebecca Hussey, Fractional Operations
- URL: https://www.rebeccahussey.com/services
- Serves: small growing teams that feel stretched thin.
- Hero: "Fractional Operations Support." Subhead: "Structure, systems, and support for growing teams."
- CTA: "Get Started" in nav and after sections.
- Trust: none visible on this page.
- Structure: hero, what I do, offer with 5 bullets, "Ideal if..." qualifier, CTA. Separate Rates page in the menu.
- Notable: a "Rates" page in the main menu. The copy talks about the owner's pain, not features.

## 6. Eli Schwartz, SEO consultant (national)
- URL: https://www.elischwartz.co
- Serves: B2B/B2C companies wanting organic growth.
- Hero: "the Product-Led SEO expert" (no subhead), with a headshot.
- CTA: "Book a call" and "Contact" in the nav; "Contact me" under bio; "Get in touch" mid-page.
- Trust: clients named in text (Shutterstock, Gusto, WordPress, Zendesk...), press logos (TechCrunch, HubSpot, Search Engine Land), a CMO quote, "Most influential SEO experts" list, plus a book and course.
- Pricing: none. Lead magnet: newsletter only.
- Notable: his credibility is borrowed from press and his book. Low proof of results, high proof of reputation.

## 7. Matthew Edgar (Texas-based technical SEO consultant)
- URL: https://www.matthewedgar.net
- Serves: companies with technical SEO / AI-search problems.
- Hero: "Matthew Edgar." Subhead: "Technical SEO, AI Search & Agent Readiness. Consultant, Author & Educator."
- CTA: "Schedule a Call" right under hero and in the closing section.
- Trust: 4 named client testimonials (Ulta Beauty, Opencare...), speaking logos (MozCon, SMX, O'Reilly), 3 books, address in footer.
- Structure: hero, topics/blog, videos, books, "Work with Matthew" (9 service bullets + testimonials), speaking, closing CTA, newsletter.
- Pricing none; lead magnet newsletter only.
- Notable: the headline is just his name, so the real pitch is buried. Do not copy that.

## 8. Dallas solo/small automation shops (the real local competition)
Both are Zapier partner directory profiles (not full websites):
- Tech Bridge Consulting, Dallas, solo. https://editor.vercel.zapier.com/partnerdirectory/partner/tech-bridge-consulting. Min spend $500, projects $501-$200,000, free discovery call, one 5.0 review.
- AI Xccelerate LLC, Dallas, 5-15 people. https://zapier.com/partnerdirectory/partner/ai-xccelerate-llc. Min spend $250, free discovery call, "fixed-price starter packs," no reviews or case studies listed.
- Takeaway: **local automation competitors are thin on proof** (one review, zero case studies) but they list a minimum price and a free discovery call. Your free-audit launch fits right in; proof is the gap you can win.

## Not found
I did not find a strong DFW solo SEO consultant with a well-built personal site. Local SEO results were agencies (BlckPanda, Plano Website Design, etc.). That is an opening, not a problem.

# PART 2: Portfolio inspiration (recruiter-facing)

Honest note: search found almost no great *operations* portfolios. These are the best proven patterns from adjacent roles.

## A. Brittany Chiang (engineer, widely praised)
- URL: https://brittanychiang.com
- Hero: "Frontend Engineer" / "I build accessible, pixel-perfect experiences for the web."
- Structure: nav anchors, about, numbered experience timeline (2015-2024), project cards with screenshots and tags, writing list, playful footer detail.
- Why it works: one-line "what I do," short timeline, every project has proof numbers (6k+ GitHub stars). Skip-to-content link.
- Borrow: the timeline + one-line headline + a tiny playful detail.

## B. Alex The Analyst (data career, non-engineer feel)
- URL: https://www.alextheanalyst.com/
- Hero: "DATA IS EVERYWHERE," then a book CTA ("Check it out!").
- Structure: nav, hero, book, social links, three offers (Consulting, Speaking, Collaborations) each with its own button, short bio, one testimonial, contact form with email fallback.
- Borrow: routing inquiries by type with separate buttons; one testimonial right before contact.

## C. Kelly Adams (data analyst/educator)
- URL: https://www.kellyjadams.com/
- Hero: "Kelly Adams" / "I create courses on data analytics and data engineering." One CTA: "VIEW COURSES."
- Why it works: one sentence, one button, numbers on every offer (hours, problem counts), article titles that state results ("How I Saved 10 Minutes with a Python Script").
- Borrow: result-style titles for your case studies.

## D. Tobias van Schneider (designer, for storytelling structure)
- URL: https://vanschneider.com
- Hero: "I CREATE; THEREFORE I AM" / "DESIGN PRAXIS EST. 2000."
- Structure: grouped nav, bold headline, bio, repeating case-study cards (client + goal, tags, 2-3 sentence summary, one link, images), essays, artifacts grid.
- Borrow: the repeating case-study card format and the strong point-of-view line. Closest to your "editorial magazine" idea. Do not copy the image-heavy design; your work is documents, not visuals.

Extra lists to browse: https://www.founderjar.com/inspiration/data-analytics-portfolio-examples/ and https://vanschneider.com/blog/portfolio-tips/how-to-make-a-non-design-portfolio/ (advice: avoid image-heavy templates when your work is text-based).

# PART 3: Patterns, ranked by impact

| # | Pattern (who does it) | Apply to Brick by Brick |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Headline names the customer's pain, not the service.** Systems Studio, OpsKings, Savvy ("Design a Company That Doesn't Depend on You," "everything runs through you") | Replace service-list hero with one pain line, e.g. "Your business runs on you. Let's fix that." Subhead names SEO, Ops, AI in one sentence. |
| 2 | **One repeated CTA with a low-risk label** ("Book an Audit," "Free Discovery Call"). Asamby repeats it 5+ times | One button everywhere: "Claim a free audit (5 spots)". Put it in hero, after services, after process, footer. |
| 3 | **A free quiz/diagnostic for people not ready to book.** Systems Studio Vacation Test, Asamby quiz | Build a 10-question "How much is your business running on you?" quiz. Best lead magnet for your audience. |
| 4 | **Show the problem list ("Is this you?").** Asamby, OpsKings, Systems Studio | Add 4-5 gut-level pains in plain words (answering every call yourself, nothing written down, etc.). |
| 5 | **A named, numbered method.** OpsKings (Audit, Map, Analyze, Execute), Asamby 4 steps | Show "Audit, Fix, Document, Automate" as 3-4 numbered steps with what the client gets at each. |
| 6 | **Proof near the top.** Logos, press, named testimonials, numbers | You have few clients. Use your own work: Covered Care results, tools you built, real before/after numbers, real photo of you. Run the free audits and ask for a quote from each. |
| 7 | **Say who it is NOT for.** OpsKings "fit / not fit" | Add "Not for corporate teams who want a 60-page deck." Fits your "not buttoned-up" brand and filters bad leads. |
| 8 | **FAQ that kills objections.** Savvy (vs. virtual assistant), Asamby (vs. consultant, first 30 days) | 6 questions: how is this different from a freelancer, what does the audit include, what do I need to give you, why free, what does it cost after. |
| 9 | **Price anchoring or visible starting point.** Zapier local shops list $250-$500 minimums; OpsKings compares to a $200K COO; Systems Studio shows a $147 product | Put "Audits from $500 (free for the first 5)" on the page. Most competitors hide price, so showing it is a trust edge. |
| 10 | **Real human, first person.** Systems Studio "I am operational, not theoretical," Asamby "No AI slop" | Real photo of Aamir in the hero or about. Write "I", not "we". Mention Plano / DFW. |
| 11 | **A guarantee or risk reducer.** Asamby 30-day money-back | "If the audit finds nothing worth fixing, you owe nothing." Easy since it is free for now. |
| 12 | **Case-study cards with a repeatable format.** van Schneider | Even with no clients: 2-3 "what I built" cards (client/problem, what I did, result). |

## Quick wins (an afternoon each, copy/layout only)
1. Rewrite hero headline around the pain (#1).
2. One CTA label, repeated in 4 places (#2).
3. Add "Is this you?" pains block (#4).
4. Add "Not for you if..." block (#7).
5. Add FAQ of 6 (#8).
6. Show "Audits from $500, first 5 free" plus a "spots left" counter (#9, scarcity like Asamby's "1-2 clients per quarter").
7. Real photo + first-person bio line (#10).
8. Add guarantee line (#11).

## Bigger lifts
1. Build the diagnostic quiz with an emailed result (#3). Biggest single upgrade.
2. Turn the 3 services into a numbered method page with deliverables (#5).
3. Collect 3-5 real testimonials/results from the free audits, then add a proof section with numbers (#6). Takes weeks, not hours.
4. 2-3 case-study cards, then a blog post per audit finding (#12). Helps SEO too.
5. A Zapier-directory profile (cheap, shows how local competitors get found) with a free discovery call listed.

## Do NOT copy
- Name-only hero (Matthew Edgar, Eli Schwartz): fine for famous people, bad for you.
- Hiding everything behind "apply" or "contact" with zero proof (Rebecca Hussey, Savvy).
- Fake or unclear proof: Systems Studio's testimonial mentions a name that is never explained; Asamby's counters showed 0. Only claim what is real.
- Targeting $2M-$100M companies and writing "we" (OpsKings, Asamby). You are a solo serving small businesses.
- Heavy corporate wording ("operational leadership", "scalable infrastructure"). Your brand is gritty and plain.
- Too many CTAs (Eli Schwartz has overlapping Book/Contact/Hire me). Keep one.
- Stock photos of empty desks (Systems Studio). A real face beats them.

# PART 4: Portfolio patterns and actions

1. **One plain sentence under your name** (Kelly Adams, Brittany Chiang). Action: "Client onboarding analyst. I turn messy handoffs into clean, automated processes."
2. **One primary button** (Kelly: "VIEW COURSES"). Action: "See my work" or "Download resume," pick one.
3. **Numbers on everything** (Kelly's hours/problems, Brittany's stars). Action: attach a number to each project (days cut, accounts onboarded, errors reduced). Use only figures you can back up and that your employer allows.
4. **Numbered experience timeline** (Brittany). Action: short role summaries, 2 lines each, with skills tags (Salesforce, SOPs, n8n).
5. **Repeatable case-study card** (van Schneider): problem, what I did, result, tools. Action: 3 cards (e.g. the onboarding workflow map, receptionist engine, Covered dashboard). Strip confidential details.
6. **Route different visitors with separate buttons** (Alex The Analyst). Action: "Hiring me" (resume) vs "Working with me" (Brick by Brick).
7. **One testimonial before the contact form** (Alex). Action: a manager or peer quote.
8. **Visible contact + email fallback.** Action: email and LinkedIn in header, no form-only contact.
9. **One small playful detail** (Brittany's TARDIS). Action: one editorial touch, e.g. a magazine "issue number" or pull-quote.
10. **Do not force pictures.** Non-design portfolios work better text-first with clean layout; use screenshots only of real work you may show.

Quick wins: 1, 2, 4, 8. Bigger lifts: 3 and 5 (writing the case studies), 7 (getting the quote).
